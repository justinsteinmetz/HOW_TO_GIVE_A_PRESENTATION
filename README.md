# How to Give a Presentation

**A cross-grade presentation skills framework for Deutsche Schule Prag students in Grades 5–12.**

A single-page interactive guide built around one principle: the same eight disciplines are revisited every year, at increasing sophistication. Students at any stage can use the same tool — the framework scales from Show & Tell to academic and professional delivery.

https://justinsteinmetz.github.io/how_to_give_a_presentation/

---

## What it is

A self-contained study guide and reference tool. No teacher setup required. Students open it independently before a presentation, during preparation, or as a self-assessment tool after the fact.

It is not a lesson. It is a framework students return to at every stage of their education whenever they are asked to speak in front of an audience.

---

## Structure

The guide is organised into six sections, accessible via the top navigation bar:

| Section | What it contains |
|---|---|
| Framework | Five stages of mastery — expandable cards showing how expectations develop from primary to graduation |
| Skills | Eight core disciplines — each one shown across all five stages |
| Checklist | A pre-presentation checklist students tick as they prepare |
| Rubric | A self-assessment and peer-feedback rubric, differentiated by stage |
| Phrases | A bank of spoken transition phrases — tap to copy, adapt before use |
| Practice | Timed speaking activities with a built-in countdown timer |

---

## The five stages

| Stage | Label | Approximate level |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | Show & Tell | Grades 1–4 |
| Stage 2 | Organised | Grades 5–7 |
| Stage 3 | Engager | Grades 8–9 |
| Stage 4 | Academic | Grades 10–12 |
| Stage 5 | Professional | Graduation+ |

The default view opens at Stage 1. Students select their stage using the tab strip at the top of the page.

---

## The eight presentation disciplines

Each discipline is presented at all five stages. Students select a discipline to see how it develops:

1. Planning
2. Structure
3. Slides
4. Voice
5. Nerves
6. Engagement
7. Questions
8. Reflection

---

## Classroom use

**As a preparation reference** — Direct students to open the guide before a presentation task. They select their stage, read the relevant disciplines, and work through the checklist as they prepare.

**As a self-assessment tool** — After presenting, students use the rubric to assess their own performance against stage-appropriate criteria.

**As a peer-feedback tool** — The rubric is structured for peer use. Students mark Beginning / Developing / Proficient / Excellent against each criterion.

**As a practice tool** — The timed activities section provides focused speaking practice tasks. The built-in timer (1 min, 2 min, 3 min, 5 min, 8 min) supports structured independent rehearsal and supports techniques like impromptu speaking and timed delivery.

---

## Differentiation

The framework is designed to be used simultaneously across a mixed-ability class. Students work within their own stage without the tool exposing that differentiation visually. A Grade 6 student using Stage 2 and a Grade 6 student using Stage 3 are looking at the same interface — only the content differs.
